# Product Marketing Context

**Document version:** v2
**Last updated:** 2026-08-08

## Product Overview
**One-liner:** Soko Mkononi is Kenya’s agricultural marketplace — the market in your hand — connecting farmers with buyers of livestock, crops, machinery, and farm inputs.
**What it does:** Farmers and agri-sellers list farm goods; buyers browse by category, order online or via WhatsApp, and get delivery across Kenya’s 47 counties. Multi-seller catalog with cart, checkout, and direct chat.
**Product category:** Agricultural marketplace / agri-commerce (how customers search: “buy dairy goats Kenya”, “farm machinery marketplace”, “livestock for sale Kenya”)
**Product type:** Multi-seller e-commerce marketplace (B2B + B2C agri)
**Business model:** Hybrid agri commerce — (1) **marketplace** for limited-supply goods (livestock, fresh produce) with commission + premium listings; (2) **retail store** for constant-supply inputs/machinery with markup. WhatsApp-assisted conversion; COD + Loop payments.

## Target Audience
**Target companies:** Smallholder and commercial farmers, agri-dealers, cooperatives, households buying livestock/produce, agribusinesses sourcing inputs
**Decision-makers:** Farm owners, procurement officers, household buyers, youth agripreneurs
**Primary use case:** Find trusted farm goods (or sell them) without relying only on middlemen or informal WhatsApp forwards
**Jobs to be done:**
- Buy healthy livestock, crops, machinery, or inputs at fair prices
- Reach buyers beyond the local market day
- Order and arrange delivery without traveling to multiple farms
**Use cases:**
- Dairy farmer sourcing goats or cattle breeding stock
- Buyer ordering poultry, eggs, or horticulture produce
- Agribusiness sourcing machinery or soil inputs countrywide

## Problems & Pain Points
**Core problem:** Farm trade is fragmented — middlemen take margin, quality is hard to verify, and buyers/sellers can’t easily find each other beyond the local soko.
**Why alternatives fall short:**
- Physical markets: limited selection, travel cost, price opacity
- Random WhatsApp/Facebook posts: no catalog, no trust layer, no delivery system
- Single-farm shops: narrow inventory, not a true marketplace
**What it costs them:** Lost income, overtime spent searching, risk of diseased or misrepresented stock
**Emotional tension:** Fear of being cheated; stress of “who can I trust with my money and my farm?”

## Competitive Landscape
**Direct:** Other agri marketplaces / digital farm shops — fall short when trust, WhatsApp-native ordering, or Kenya-wide category breadth is weak
**Secondary:** Brokers and local markets — fall short on transparency, reach, and convenience
**Indirect:** Informal social media selling — falls short on structure, discovery, and fulfillment

## Differentiation
**Key differentiators:**
- Brand promise in the name: *Soko Mkononi* — the market in your hand
- Broad agri categories (livestock → crops → horticulture → machinery → inputs)
- WhatsApp-first ordering that matches how Kenyans already buy
- Multi-seller ready with countrywide delivery messaging
**How we do it differently:** One browseable marketplace plus one-tap chat — not only ads, not only a single farm storefront
**Why that's better:** Faster discovery, clearer prices, less travel, more trust
**Why customers choose us:** Convenience + trust + breadth of farm goods

## Objections
| Objection | Response |
|-----------|----------|
| “How do I know the animals/products are good?” | Ask sellers on WhatsApp, check listings and reviews, and work with vaccinated/registered stock where listed |
| “Is delivery real outside Nairobi?” | We arrange countrywide delivery — typically 3–7 working days |

**Anti-persona:** Buyers who only want ultra-cheap unverified stock with zero documentation; sellers who refuse any digital ordering

## Goals
**Business goal:** Become Kenya’s go-to agricultural marketplace connecting farmers and buyers
**Conversion action:** Browse → WhatsApp/order (primary); complete checkout (secondary)
**Current metrics:** Early hybrid build — multi-seller catalog, commerce modes, commission snapshots, timed featured listings; seller self-service / escrow / auctions still ahead

## Hybrid model phases (Soko Mkononi)
1. **Now** — Encode marketplace vs retail on products; commission on marketplace seller lines; timed featured listings; one checkout
2. **Next** — Seller login + listing self-service; cost/markup fields for retail SKUs; payout ledger (manual then automated)
3. **Trust** — Escrow / release-on-delivery; stronger verification for livestock lots
4. **Logistics** — Warehousing for retail SKUs; coordinated farm pickup for marketplace lots
5. **Finance** — Input credit, insurance referrals, analytics for B2B buyers
6. **Trade** — Export lanes and cross-border buyers (only after domestic liquidity)

## Programmatic SEO (priority playbooks)
1. **Directory / category hubs** — `/shop/{category}` for livestock, horticulture, machinery, inputs
2. **Locations** — “buy [product] in [county]” pages once inventory & demand justify
3. **Personas** — “marketplace for dairy farmers / poultry keepers”
4. **Examples / guides** — buying guides that feed trust (disease-free livestock checklist)

## Marketing ideas (early stage)
1. Easy keyword ranking on category + product intent
2. WhatsApp / Facebook farmer communities (native where the audience already is)
3. Seller referrals (farmer brings farmer)
4. Comparison content vs brokers (trust + price transparency)
5. Short-form farm video → product links
